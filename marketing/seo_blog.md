**Keywords:** study timetable for students, smart timetable generator, topper study schedule, how to plan for exams.

---

Every student knows the feeling: you spend 2 hours making a beautiful, color-coded timetable, only to abandon it by the second day. 

Why does this happen? Most timetables are **aspirational, not realistic.** They don't account for energy levels, subject difficulty, or real-life interruptions.

In this guide, we’ll show you how to build a "Smart Timetable" that sticks.

### 1. The Weightage Secret
Don't give every subject 1 hour. Use the **Priority Weighting** method. If Math is 2x harder for you than English, it needs 2x the time. Our [Smart Timetable Generator](LINK_TO_YOUR_TOOL) does this calculation for you automatically.

### 2. Deep Work vs. Shallow Work
Schedule your hardest subjects (Deep Work) when your brain is freshest—usually early morning. Save "Shallow Work" (like organizing notes or checking emails) for your afternoon slump.

### 3. The 90-Minute Rule
Research shows our brains can only focus intensely for about 90 minutes. Break your study sessions into 90-minute blocks followed by a 15-minute "brain break."
